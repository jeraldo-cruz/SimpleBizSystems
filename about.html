Keeping Local Business Strong in a Growing Hill Country

Kingsland and the surrounding Highland Lakes region are expanding quickly. With new residents, new construction, and increased tourism across Llano and Burnet Counties, the business landscape is shifting rapidly.

Our core mission is to make sure our local small businesses thrive as Kingsland grows. The electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, boat mechanics, food trucks, and family-owned storefronts are the backbone of this lake community. When rapid growth happens, local operations shouldn't get pushed out or outmatched simply because big regional corporate franchises have massive software budgets.

We build lean, simple digital workflows and management tools that give independent business owners an unfair advantage—saving them hours of paperwork, keeping customer follow-ups on track, and letting them run professional operations without expensive monthly retainers.

About Simple Biz Systems & Founder Jeraldo Cruz

Simple Biz Systems is based right here in Kingsland, Texas. Founded by Jeraldo Cruz, the practice was born out of a simple observation: modern business software has become needlessly complicated, overpriced, and bloated with features that local operators never use.

With a deep technical background in operations management, systems engineering, and workflow architecture—including years supporting mission-critical enterprise systems and high-volume operations—Jeraldo translates technical best practices into lightweight, easy-to-use tools for local owners.

Whether it is a shift roster that works seamlessly on mobile, an automated profit and estimate calculator, or an ad-free public utility wire for our neighbors, the philosophy is always the same: keep it simple, keep it fast, and keep it useful.

The Public Utility: The Kingsland Wire

As part of our commitment to Kingsland, we created and maintain the Kingsland Wire as a 100% free, ad-free public bulletin.

Most commercial news sites today are filled with paywalls, invasive tracking cookies, and screen-blocking popups. The Wire runs on lean, automated infrastructure that aggregates verified data directly from official sources—including the National Weather Service, County Emergency Management burn ban orders, Texas Water Development Board lake gauges, TxDOT road closures, and Chamber of Commerce events.

It is proof of what lightweight, thoughtful technology can do for a community when user respect comes first.
